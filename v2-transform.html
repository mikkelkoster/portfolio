The transformation — five treatments

The content is five matched pairs, but the section presents them as two independent five-item lists. The reader has to pair them up themselves, and most will not — which is why it reads flat. Every treatment below makes the pairing the structure.

01Paired rows

One row per pair, so each change is read across rather than down. Grey on the left, ink on the right — the same step the icons and bullets already make. Closest to what is there now, and the smallest change that fixes the actual problem.

02Struck through

The old way crossed out, the new way after it, on one line. The most direct of the five — you cannot misread what replaced what. It is also the boldest thing on the sheet, which may be one device too many for a build this quiet.

03Ledger

No cards, no arrows, no icons — two columns and a hairline per pair. The quietest option and the most editorial. It does reintroduce rules, which the sheet has just finished removing everywhere else.

04Not this, but this

Each pair written as one sentence rather than two fragments, and set larger. Five sentences instead of ten bullets. It reads as writing rather than as a table, and it is the only one where the section could carry no other furniture at all.

The transformation

Design moved from order‑taker to strategic partner.

No longer handed specs — design co-creates the strategy and the roadmap.

No longer executing UI requests — design drives the research the decisions rest on.

No longer outside the roadmap — design has direct input into platform vision and OKRs.

No longer brought in when needed — design is at the table before the decision is made.

No longer measured in screens — design is measured in outcomes.

05The step up

The before sits small and grey, the after sits below it, larger and indented behind a short mark — so each pair is literally a step up the page. Uses position rather than colour to carry the change, and needs no arrows.
